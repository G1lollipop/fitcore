Title: Nutrient Timing (Carbohydrate & Protein)
Source: ISSN Position Stand — Nutrient Timing (Kerksick et al., 2017)
URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5596471/
DOI: 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4
License: CC BY 4.0 (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition)
Tags: nutrient timing; carbohydrate; protein; glycogen; recovery; meal frequency

Summary (evidence-based position of the International Society of Sports Nutrition):

1. Nutrient timing — planned intake of whole foods, fortified foods, and supplements — can enhance recovery and tissue repair, augment muscle protein synthesis (MPS), and improve mood after high-volume or intense exercise.

2. Glycogen stores are maximized by a high-carbohydrate diet (8–12 g carbohydrate/kg/day) and are depleted most by high-volume exercise.

3. If glycogen must be restored quickly (< 4 h recovery), consider: aggressive carbohydrate refeeding (1.2 g/kg/h, favoring high-glycemic >70 sources); adding caffeine (3–8 mg/kg); or combining carbohydrate (0.8 g/kg/h) with protein (0.2–0.4 g/kg/h).

4. During long (>60 min) high-intensity exercise, consume ~30–60 g carbohydrate/h in a 6–8% carbohydrate-electrolyte solution every 10–15 min. If carbohydrate intake is inadequate, adding protein can help performance and reduce muscle damage.

5. Carbohydrate during resistance training promotes stable blood glucose and higher glycogen; combined with protein it can reduce muscle damage and improve adaptations.

6. Meeting total daily protein — ideally as evenly spaced feedings (~every 3 h) — should be the primary emphasis for exercising individuals.

7. About 10 g of essential amino acids (free form or within a 20–40 g protein bolus) maximally stimulates MPS.

8. Pre- and/or post-exercise protein (with or without carbohydrate) supports strength and body-composition gains. The size/timing of a pre-workout meal affects how much post-workout protein is needed.

9. A 20–40 g protein dose (0.25–0.40 g/kg/dose) every 3–4 hours most favorably affects MPS and is linked to better body composition and performance.

10. Casein (~30–40 g) before sleep acutely raises overnight MPS and metabolic rate without impairing fat breakdown.

Practical takeaways:
- Total daily intake (calories, protein, carbs) is the foundation; timing is the fine-tuning on top.
- The "anabolic window" is wider than once believed — getting protein within a few hours of training is sufficient for most.
- Time carbs around hard or long sessions for performance and recovery.
