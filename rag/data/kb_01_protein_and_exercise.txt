Title: Protein Intake for Exercising Individuals
Source: ISSN Position Stand — Protein and Exercise (Jäger et al., 2017)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/
DOI: 10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8
License: CC BY 4.0 (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition)
Tags: protein; muscle protein synthesis; hypertrophy; fat loss; recovery; nutrition

Summary (evidence-based position of the International Society of Sports Nutrition):

1. Resistance exercise and protein ingestion both stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and are synergistic when protein is consumed shortly before or after training.

2. For building and maintaining muscle, an overall daily protein intake of 1.4–2.0 g protein per kg body weight per day is sufficient for most exercising individuals. This falls within the Institute of Medicine's Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range.

3. Higher intakes of 2.3–3.1 g/kg/day may be needed to preserve lean body mass in resistance-trained people during a calorie deficit (cutting/fat loss).

4. There is novel evidence that intakes above 3.0 g/kg/day may improve body composition (more fat loss) in resistance-trained individuals, with no harm to healthy people.

5. To maximize MPS per meal, aim for roughly 0.25 g/kg body weight per serving, or an absolute dose of 20–40 g of high-quality protein.

6. Each protein dose should ideally contain 700–3000 mg of leucine plus a full spread of essential amino acids (EAAs).

7. Distribute protein evenly across the day, roughly every 3–4 hours.

8. Timing is flexible: the anabolic effect of a training session lasts at least 24 hours, though it diminishes as time passes. Pre- or post-workout protein both work — choose what fits your tolerance and schedule.

9. Whole foods can meet protein needs, but supplements (e.g., whey, casein) are a practical way to hit quality and quantity targets while controlling calories.

10. Rapidly digested proteins high in EAAs and leucine are most effective at stimulating MPS. Prioritize complete protein sources that contain all EAAs.

11. Endurance athletes should first secure adequate carbohydrate for performance; added protein can help offset muscle damage and support recovery.

12. A pre-sleep dose of casein (30–40 g) increases overnight MPS and metabolic rate without impairing fat breakdown.

Practical takeaways:
- Most lifters: target ~1.6 g/kg/day as a solid default; go higher (up to ~2.4 g/kg) when dieting to protect muscle.
- Spread intake across 3–5 meals of 20–40 g each.
- Don't obsess over a narrow "anabolic window"; total daily protein and consistency matter most.

Disclaimer: General fitness/nutrition education, not medical advice. People with kidney disease or other medical conditions should consult a physician before changing protein intake.
