Title: Caffeine and Exercise Performance
Source: ISSN Position Stand — Caffeine and Exercise Performance (Guest et al., 2021)
URL: https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4
DOI: 10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4
License: CC BY 4.0 (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition)
Tags: caffeine; ergogenic aid; endurance; strength; dosing; pre-workout

Summary (evidence-based position of the International Society of Sports Nutrition):

1. Caffeine acutely improves many aspects of exercise performance: muscular endurance, strength, movement velocity, sprinting, jumping, throwing, and a wide range of aerobic and anaerobic sport-specific actions (benefits are small to moderate and not universal across all studies).

2. Effective dose is 3–6 mg/kg body mass. Minimal effective doses may be as low as 2 mg/kg. Very high doses (e.g., 9 mg/kg) do not add benefit and raise side-effect risk.

3. Most common timing is ~60 min before exercise for caffeine in capsule/anhydrous form. Optimal timing depends on the source — e.g., caffeine chewing gum may act faster.

4. Caffeine is ergogenic for both trained and untrained individuals.

5. Caffeine benefits endurance exercise in heat and at altitude (well supported at 3–6 mg/kg, and 4–6 mg/kg respectively).

6. Individual responses vary due to genetics (e.g., CYP1A2, ADORA2A) and habitual intake; some people are non-responders or experience side effects (anxiety, jitters, GI upset, disrupted sleep).

7. Anhydrous caffeine (capsules, powder) is more reliably ergogenic than coffee, though coffee can also be effective.

8. Caffeine can improve cognitive aspects of performance such as attention and alertness, especially when sleep-deprived.

Practical takeaways:
- A typical pre-workout dose is ~3 mg/kg (e.g., ~200 mg for a 70 kg person) taken ~45–60 min before training.
- Avoid late-day caffeine to protect sleep (which itself drives recovery and performance).
- Find your personal tolerance; more is not better.
